Debt Snowball vs Avalanche: Which Method Gets You Free Faster?

If you're trying to pay off debt, you've probably heard of two main strategies: the Debt Snowball and the Debt Avalanche. Both work. Both have helped thousands of people get completely out of debt. The question is which one works better for you.

What is the Debt Snowball?

The Debt Snowball method, made popular by financial expert Dave Ramsey, focuses on paying off your smallest debt first regardless of interest rate. You make minimum payments on everything else and throw every extra dollar at your smallest balance. When that's gone you move to the next smallest and so on.

The psychology behind it is powerful. You get quick wins early. Paying off a debt completely feels amazing and keeps you motivated to keep going. Studies have shown that people who use this method are more likely to stick with their debt payoff plan all the way to the end.

What is the Debt Avalanche?

The Debt Avalanche method is mathematically smarter. Instead of targeting the smallest balance, you target the debt with the highest interest rate first. You still make minimum payments on everything else but your extra money goes toward the most expensive debt first.

Over time this approach saves you more money in interest and technically gets you out of debt faster on paper. The downside is it can take longer to see your first debt fully paid off which can feel discouraging for some people.

Which Should You Use?

Here's the honest answer: the best method is the one you will actually stick to. If motivation is your biggest challenge use the Snowball. If you're disciplined and want to save the most money use the Avalanche.

Many people start with the Snowball to build momentum and then switch to the Avalanche once they're in the habit. That's a completely valid approach.

Getting Started Today

  • List all your debts with their balances and interest rates
  • Choose your method: smallest balance first or highest interest first
  • Set up minimum payments on all debts automatically
  • Direct every extra dollar to your target debt
  • Celebrate every debt you eliminate — it matters

The most important thing is to start. Both methods beat doing nothing by a massive margin.

Zero Based Budgeting: How to Give Every Dollar a Job

Most budgets fail because they're vague. You know roughly what you earn and you try not to spend too much. Zero based budgeting is different. It's precise, intentional, and it works even when money is extremely tight.

What is Zero Based Budgeting?

Zero based budgeting means your income minus your expenses equals zero. Every single dollar you earn gets assigned a specific purpose before the month begins. That doesn't mean you spend everything — saving and debt payoff are part of the budget too. It means every dollar has a job.

How to Set It Up

  • Step 1: Write down your total monthly income after tax
  • Step 2: List every fixed expense — rent, utilities, subscriptions, minimum debt payments
  • Step 3: List variable expenses — food, transport, clothing, entertainment
  • Step 4: Add a savings line and a debt payoff line
  • Step 5: Keep subtracting until you reach exactly zero

What If There's Not Enough?

If your expenses are more than your income, something has to go. Start with subscriptions you can cut, eating out, and any non-essential spending. It won't feel good at first but this is the process that actually changes your situation.

Free Tools to Help

You don't need fancy software. A free spreadsheet works perfectly. We have a free budget template available on our tools page that you can download and start using today. Apps like EveryDollar also offer a free version built specifically for zero based budgeting.

The goal isn't perfection in month one. The goal is awareness. Once you know exactly where your money is going you have the power to change it.

5 Apps That Automatically Save Money For You

The hardest part of saving money is remembering to do it. These apps solve that problem by automating the process entirely. Set them up once and your savings grow while you focus on everything else.

1. Acorns

Acorns rounds up your everyday purchases to the nearest dollar and invests the difference. Spend $3.60 on coffee and Acorns invests $0.40 automatically. It adds up faster than you'd expect and you barely notice it leaving your account. Great for complete beginners with investing.

2. Chime

Chime's Save When You Spend feature automatically transfers a percentage of every purchase into your savings account. It's a free banking app with no fees and no minimum balance. Their round-up feature works similarly to Acorns but keeps the money in savings rather than investing it.

3. Digit

Digit analyses your spending patterns and automatically moves small amounts into savings when it determines you can afford it. It's smart enough not to overdraft your account. Perfect for people who struggle to save because there's never an obvious amount to put aside.

4. Qapital

Qapital lets you set custom saving rules. Save every time you skip eating out. Save a fixed amount every Friday. Save when your favourite sports team wins. You set the rules and it handles the rest automatically.

5. Ally Bank

Not a savings app exactly but Ally's high yield savings account pays significantly more interest than traditional banks. Set up an automatic weekly transfer of even $5 and watch compound interest do its work over time. The higher interest rate makes a real difference over months and years.

You don't need to use all five. Pick one that fits how you already spend and set it up today. Automating even a small amount of saving is infinitely better than saving nothing.
